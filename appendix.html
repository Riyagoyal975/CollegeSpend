Mathematics in Action

Discrete Math Pillars

Theoretical computer science principles applied directly to student personal finance:

● 1. Set Theory |A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B|

Models category universes. Computes Union, Intersection, Difference ($A \setminus B$), and verifies the Principle of Inclusion-Exclusion dynamically.

● 2. Binary Relations M_R ∈ {0, 1}⁷ˣ⁷ | M_R = M_Rᵀ

Evaluates category co-occurrences. Validates Reflexive ($a R a$), Symmetric ($a R b \implies b R a$), and Transitive properties across spending sets.

● 3. Functions & Mapping f: E → C | (h ∘ g ∘ f)(x)

Maps expenses to categories. Evaluates Injectivity (Many-to-One Pigeonhole Principle), Surjectivity, and calculates composite cost-residue functions.

● 4. Propositional Logic P, Q, R, S | (P ∨ ¬P) ≡ T

Converts live balance and allowance thresholds into atomic propositions; dynamically generates $2^n$-row truth tables and tautology proofs.
